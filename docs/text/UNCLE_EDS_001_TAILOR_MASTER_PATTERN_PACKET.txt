UNCLE ED'S 001
ONE OF ONE — THE LEGACY TUXEDO
TAILOR MASTER PATTERN & CUTTING PACKET
By Edris Nazparvar · 2026
IMPORTANT: This packet is the definitive design and drafting specification, but it is not yet a mathematically complete 1:1 production
pattern. The tailor must verify the missing body measurements, draft the full-scale paper pattern, and make a toile before cutting the final
cloth.
1. Definitive jacket design
• Structured Boxy Hollywood fit: strong controlled mass, not slim and not oversized.
• Crossover single-button architecture with one functional closure and controlled overlap. Main button: aged silver, 25–28 mm, Uncle Ed’s
precision-machined logo.
• 12 cm peak lapel, high gorge, sharp peak, completely straight outer edge. No bowed or scooped lapel line. Black satin facing.
• Architectural natural shoulder, controlled pad, high armhole, full chest, full canvas. Long clean double vents.
• Exterior pockets jetted with no flaps. Interior pockets concealed and functional.
• Sleeve slightly fuller than contemporary slim; articulated elbow; four functional aged-silver cuff buttons; 1.5–2 cm shirt cuff visible.
2. Shirt, bow tie and trousers
• White formal shirt, French cuffs, aged-silver studs. Large black satin butterfly bow tie.
• High-rise flat-front trousers. Finished waistband target 4.5 cm; absolute maximum 5 cm. Side adjusters, no belt loops, concealed
hook-and-bar.
• Fuller thigh, controlled taper, minimal break. No satin side stripe. Jetted back pockets.

6. Required markings on every pattern piece
• Piece number and name; cut quantity; material ID; grainline; center-front/center-back or fold line; notches and balance marks;
pocket/button locations; seam and hem allowances; lapel roll/break line; construction reference marks.
• Add a 10 cm calibration square to the master drafting sheet and every tiled print page if the pattern is later digitized for A4 output.
7. Lapel drafting law
• Finished width exactly 12.0 cm at the defined measurement point.
• High gorge and sharp peak. Outer edge must remain straight and architectural between defined points.
• Establish button position and lapel break together so the crossover front remains balanced.
• Draft facing separately and verify the finished pressed width remains 12 cm. Check left/right symmetry before cutting.
8. Construction
• Full canvas. Hand-shaped chest. Hand-padded lapels. Controlled shoulder pad and sleeve head, never military-looking. High armhole.
Precise sleeve pitch from actual posture. Reinforce armholes, pocket openings, button attachment, vents, crotch and waistband. Make a
toile and transfer corrections to the master pattern.
9. Trousers pattern
• Front rise recorded 38 cm and back rise 40 cm — verify on body. Waistband finished 4.5 cm target / 5 cm maximum. High natural waist.
Side adjusters, no belt loops. Calf 43 cm and ankle 26 cm recorded — verify both legs. Outseam 100 cm recorded — verify with shoes.
Minimal break.
10. Materials and identity
• Outer: deepest black wool, approximately 280–320 g/m², controlled sheen, excellent drape and enough structure for architecture. Subtle
deep-green depth only if it reads black.
• Lining: black/deep forest cupro or Bemberg with Persian geometric logic, Art Deco geometry and organic branching/circular pathways;
hidden oxblood accents.
• Hardware: aged silver. No military insignia, shoulder boards, tactical straps, decorative ammunition-like hardware or historical uniform
symbols.
• Label: UNCLE ED’S / By Edris Nazparvar / 001 / ONE OF ONE / 2026.

11. Hidden functional system
• Concealed phone, wallet, passport and valuables compartments; pen slot; small emergency tailoring/repair compartment. Nothing
utility-like visible outside.
• Hidden inscriptions: THE BODY IS NATURE. / THE MIND IS CIVILIZATION. / DEATH COMPLETES THE CIRCLE.
12. Cutting order
• 1) Re-measure body. 2) Draft jacket and trouser blocks. 3) Convert to 001 design. 4) Draft shell, facing, canvas, lining and sleeve
pieces. 5) Add allowances and markings. 6) Check symmetry and balance. 7) Cut toile. 8) Fit and correct shoulder, chest, waist, sleeve
pitch, jacket length, lapel break and trouser rise. 9) Transfer corrections to master paper pattern. 10) Cut final cloth.
FINAL DESIGN LAW
Nothing looks accidental. The finished garment must read as an exceptionally refined black evening tuxedo. The engineering is
internal; the visible result is luxury tailoring. The outside creates intrigue. The inside rewards curiosity.
