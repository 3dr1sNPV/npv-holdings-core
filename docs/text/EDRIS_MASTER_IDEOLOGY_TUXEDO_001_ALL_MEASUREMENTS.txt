1. EDRIS ideology

Core statement: “We civilize the wild. We build against time. We know everything eventually returns to nature. So we make something worth leaving behind.”

Master idea: civilization against nature, created in the face of mortality. The cycle is nature → life → desire → civilization → creation → decay → death → return to nature. Death completes the cycle. Humans build clothing, architecture, machines, rituals and beauty to give existence meaning. Civilization decays; the body returns to nature; an object can become an artifact, memory a story, and a story a legacy. EDRIS creates against time while acknowledging that time ultimately wins.

Hidden inscription ideas: THE BODY IS NATURE. / THE MIND IS CIVILIZATION. / DEATH COMPLETES THE CIRCLE.

One-sentence definition: EDRIS is Iranian in soul, Art Deco in structure, Gothic in atmosphere, Hollywood in presence, feral in instinct, engineered in execution, and mortal in philosophy.

ANCIENT SOUL — IRAN: Iranian history, architecture, gardens, poetry, geometry, landscapes, rain, mountains, deserts, cities, metalwork and textiles translated subtly into modern design.

DARK ARCHITECTURE: Art Deco is the structural grammar—1920s–30s symmetry, verticality, stepped forms, radiating lines, portals and machine-age elegance. Gothic ruins provide monumentality, melancholy, mystery and mortality, not medieval costume.

CINEMATIC GLAMOUR: Old Hollywood masculinity, black-tie ritual, James Bond precision and seduction, Godfather authority, Grand Budapest Hotel theatrical discipline.

THE FERAL HUMAN: instinct beneath manners—sensuality, appetite, severity, danger and nature, expressed through shape, texture and atmosphere rather than literal animal costume.

THE MACHINE: engineering, mechanical beauty, purposeful hardware, functional detail and the tension between technology and humanity.

MORTALITY: time, decay, ruins, memory, destiny, death, inheritance, artifacts and legacy.

6. Tuxedo 001 — ONE OF ONE: design brief

Identity: EDRIS 001 — ONE OF ONE, a personal bespoke tuxedo. Historical working files may say UNCLE ED’S; use EDRIS in new materials unless preserving an archival label.

Overall: modern cinematic tuxedo mixing Old Hollywood presence, Savile Row discipline, Art Deco geometry, controlled Gothic darkness and aged-silver hardware.

Cloth: deepest black luxury wool, earlier target 280–320 g/m², low-to-medium controlled sheen, strong drape and enough body for architectural lines. Forest-black undertone only if it reads black; plain black remains valid.

Jacket: long, clean, architectural single-breasted one-button dinner jacket with controlled crossover/overlap as described in the master spec. Structured boxy Hollywood fit; full chest, controlled waist, clean vertical body; neither skin-tight nor sagging oversized.

Shoulder: sculpted, slightly extended, natural rather than military; no exaggerated padding. High armhole with mobility and clean scye.

Full black satin or black grosgrain facing, controlled sheen. Balance button, break/roll line and crossover front together; press and verify finished 12 cm width and left/right symmetry. Older 11.4 cm/4.5-inch option is superseded.

Pockets: clean double-jetted/low-profile front pockets, restrained breast welt; concealed functional interior pockets must not distort exterior.

Vents: double vents, 26–28 cm starting target.

Exterior: no shoulder boards, straps, tactical webbing, military insignia or decorative utility.

Lining: black/deep-forest cupro/Bemberg, hidden Iranian geometric logic with Art Deco geometry and organic branching/circular paths, subtle asymmetry and restrained hidden oxblood accents.

11. Pattern pieces and quality control

Jacket pieces

J01 left front shell; J02 right front shell; J03 side body/side panel; J04 back shell; J05 upper sleeve; J06 under sleeve; J07 collar/neck piece; J08 left lapel/facing; J09 right lapel/facing; J10 front pocket welt components; J11 chest pocket welt; J12 vent facing/reinforcement; J13 complete lining; J14 front chest canvas; J15 shoulder reinforcement; J16 sleeve head/wadding; J17 pocket and stress-point reinforcement.

Trousers need front/back leg pieces, waistband, fly/closure components, pocket bags, side-adjuster pieces, rear welt components, waistband/interfacing and reinforcement. The tailor must assign IDs and cut quantities.

Every pattern piece must include

Piece number/name; cut quantity and mirrored status; material ID; grainline; fold/center lines; notches and balance marks; seam and hem allowances; pocket/button locations; lapel roll/break line, gorge and peak; vent ends and construction marks; scale calibration (ideally a 10 cm square).

Pieces must be clearly separated, not interwoven or overlapping. Add seam allowances outside finished dimensions. Preserve toile allowances at shoulders, side body, center back, collar, sleeve and trouser seat/rise.

Fitting and validation

Make a physical toile before cutting expensive cloth.

Fit in order: front/back balance → shoulder slope → collar/neck → chest → lapel roll → waist/side body → sleeve pitch → sleeve length → trouser rise/seat → thigh/knee/taper → hem/break.

Do not suppress the waist before balance is correct.

Check grainlines, symmetry, notches, button position, lapel width after pressing, roll/break, vents, pocket levels and cut quantities.

A large-format plotter PDF is desired, not tiled A3/A4 sheets. Previous pattern drawings were reported as abnormal/interwoven; redraw/review rather than assuming they are production-safe.

A real body cannot be fitted accurately from circumferences alone; shoulder slope, posture, arm pitch, balance and asymmetry require fitting.
