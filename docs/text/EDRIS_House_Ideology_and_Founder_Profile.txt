EDRIS

THE HOUSE IDEOLOGY

A HOUSE OF FASHION — IRAN
BUILD AGAINST TIME.

A consolidated portrait of the founder’s recorded creative preferences, personal background, cultural and artistic influences, sensory tastes, and the philosophy of House EDRIS. This is a best-effort synthesis of available saved context and project records—not a verbatim transcript of every conversation. Personal interpretations are labeled as inferences.

1. The ideology in one paragraph

EDRIS is an Iranian-rooted house about the fragile conflict between civilization and the feral human. We are born from nature, build cities, clothes, machines, rituals and beauty to give life meaning, then watch everything decay and return to nature. Death is not an interruption; it completes the cycle. What remains is the artifact, the memory, the story and the legacy. EDRIS therefore makes objects that feel beautiful, slightly uncanny, intelligently engineered and worthy of surviving their moment. Its language joins hidden Iranian soul, Art Deco structure, Gothic monumentality, Old Hollywood glamour, sensual danger and mechanical precision. Darkness is atmosphere—not a gimmick. Luxury is proven by craft, character and permanence, not logos or artificial exclusivity.

“We civilize the wild. We build against time. We know everything eventually returns to nature. So we make something worth leaving behind.”

2. Founder profile: what the conversations suggest

These are behavioral impressions from project conversations, not psychological diagnoses.

Imaginative and ambitious: tends to think in worlds, systems and long arcs rather than isolated products.

A systems-builder: wants clothing, fragrance, objects, digital tools, engineering, craft, packaging and archives to share one coherent logic.

Aesthetic and technical at once: interested in beauty, tailoring, mechanics, vehicles, product architecture, software, and commercialization.

Cinematic and atmosphere-sensitive: gravitates toward mood, character, symbolism, tension and memorable silhouettes.

Iterative and exacting: revises decisions until they match the intended result and expects established choices to carry forward.

Practical about outcomes: values tangible deliverables—usable patterns, specifications, prototypes, files, research papers and functioning systems.

Distinctiveness over trend: prefers recognizable identity and long-term value over generic luxury codes or fashionable noise.

Strong aversion to sloppiness: dislikes broken formatting, overlapping pattern pieces, curved lapel lines when a straight line is specified, sagging proportions, and designs that feel generic or costume-like.

Wants function and fashion together: an object should work well and look intentional; decoration alone is insufficient.

5. The six pillars of EDRIS

Ancient Soul — Iran

Iranian history, geometry, architecture, gardens, poetry, landscapes, metalwork and textiles. The references should be structural, poetic or hidden—not costume.

Dark Architecture

Art Deco is the structural grammar: symmetry, verticality, stepped forms, radiating geometry and machine-age elegance. Gothic ruins contribute scale, melancholy, mystery and mortality.

Cinematic Glamour

Old Hollywood’s formal presence; James Bond’s precision and seduction; Godfather authority; Grand Budapest Hotel’s theatrical discipline. Every detail should feel directed, not randomly decorated.

The Feral Human

Instinct beneath manners: sensuality, severity, appetite, danger and the natural body beneath civilization. Expressed through shape, texture and atmosphere—not literal animal costumes.

The Machine

Engineering, purposeful hardware, mechanical beauty, functional closures and the uneasy relationship between technology and humanity.

Mortality

Time, decay, ruins, memory, destiny, death, inheritance and legacy. The object outlives the moment; the archive preserves its story.

Old Hollywood and classic black-tie masculinity.

The Masked Rabbit should feel symmetrical, vertically elongated, severe and intelligent, with architectural planes, deep eyes, an angular muzzle and subtle Iranian geometry.

8. Fashion, materials and object preferences

Overall: dark, architectural, cinematic luxury; controlled proportions; strong lines; tactile materials; details that reveal themselves slowly.

Palette: midnight black, forest-black, ink, deep bottle green, midnight navy, deep burgundy, ivory, aged silver and gunmetal.

Preferred contrasts: black/white; polished/oxidized; soft wool/hard metal; ancient/futuristic; Iran/Hollywood; beauty/unease; life/death; hand/machine.

Jewelry: aged or polished silver; substantial architectural pieces; Roman/architectural luxury, Cartier-like refinement, and Chrome Hearts-like gothic/rebel silver craft. Gold and dark metal are selective accents.

Watches: Seiko SKX007, Rolex Submariner and Vacheron Constantin are reference points; desired direction is a James Bond-like tactical-dress watch.

Sneakers now included in the style DNA: Adidas Originals Adi2000, Adidas Niteball II and Yeezy 700.

Formalwear preference: Old Hollywood/Savile Row discipline with a modern silhouette; fashionable, not oversized or costume-like.

Tuxedo 001: midnight/forest-black luxury wool; long single-breasted one-button dinner jacket; sculpted but not exaggerated shoulder; controlled waist; straight 12 cm peak lapel; white formal shirt with French cuffs and clean/concealed front; aged-silver studs, buttons and cufflinks; large black bow tie; rose/pocket-square styling. Plain black remains an option; any green undertone must be extremely subtle.

Avoid curved lapel lines when a straight lapel is specified, sagging oversized proportions, unnecessary trouser stripe changes, and visual clutter.

9. House visual language

Architecture: black stone, dark wood, blackened metal, selective greenery and warm controlled light.

Materials and finishes: near-black surfaces, tactile cloth, aged silver, brushed metal, oxidized recesses, polished high points, and hidden geometric detail.

Packaging: near-black exterior, restrained aged-silver details, blind/debossed rabbit, generous typography, hidden Iranian geometry and a deliberate reveal that feels like opening an artifact chamber.

Website/app: editorial and cinematic, not a generic webshop; near-black background, ivory typography, aged-silver accents, editorial serif for stories, restrained sans-serif controls, sharp geometry and no gamified behavior.

The Masked Rabbit is a signature, not a repetitive decorative print. Use the full crest, rabbit mask or simplified silhouette according to context.

10. The design test

Beautiful at first glance.

Slightly strange at second glance.

Meaningful at third glance.

Recognizable without a logo.

Iranian in soul, never costume.

Architectural and materially convincing.

Useful or emotionally necessary.

Durable enough to become an artifact.

11. What EDRIS must never become

Generic Persian luxury or literal Persian costume.

Costume Gothic, medieval fantasy or a cheap ‘dark aesthetic’.

Literal steampunk, cyberpunk cosplay or military cosplay.

Generic streetwear, logo-covered luxury, fake aristocracy or generic quiet luxury.

Trend-chasing, movie/game merchandise, excessive skulls/gears/claws, superficial AI gimmicks or surveillance-heavy personalization.

A catalogue expanded merely to increase product count. Every item must earn its place.

12. One-sentence definition

EDRIS is Iranian in soul, Art Deco in structure, Gothic in atmosphere, Hollywood in presence, feral in instinct, engineered in execution, and mortal in philosophy.
